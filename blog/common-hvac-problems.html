Common HVAC Problems Boise Homeowners Face

Boise's climate is demanding on heating and cooling systems. Hot dry summers stress air conditioners. Long cold winters stress furnaces. Here are the most common HVAC problems, their warning signs, and when to call a licensed technician.

1. Clogged or Dirty Air Filters

This is the #1 cause of preventable HVAC breakdowns. A clogged filter restricts airflow and forces the system to work harder. Boise's dusty, dry air means filters clog faster than in wetter climates.

Warning signs: weak airflow from vents, higher energy bills, system running constantly, or a musty smell.

Fix: replace the filter every 1 to 2 months during peak seasons. See our maintenance guide.

2. Refrigerant Leaks (Low Refrigerant)

Your AC needs a specific charge of refrigerant to cool properly. If the charge drops, cooling performance falls and the compressor can overwork and fail.

Warning signs: warm air from vents, ice on the refrigerant lines, hissing sounds, or higher bills with less cooling.

Cost: leak repair plus recharge typically runs $200 to $1,500. See our AC repair page.

3. Thermostat Malfunctions

A faulty thermostat can make a good system run wrong. Many "broken" systems are simply a misconfigured or failing thermostat.

Warning signs: system won't start, temperature doesn't match the setting, or system turns on and off rapidly.

Fix: check batteries and settings first. A technician can install a replacement.

4. Frozen AC Coils

Despite the heat, evaporator coils can freeze when airflow is restricted or refrigerant is low.

Warning signs: ice on refrigerant lines, warm air, or water pooling near the indoor unit.

Action: turn off the AC immediately. Running a frozen system can damage the compressor.

5. Dirty Condenser Coils

The outdoor condenser coil releases heat. In Boise's dusty summers, the coil can clog and run hot and inefficiently.

Warning signs: AC running constantly, weak cooling, or higher bills.

Fix: keep the area clear and schedule a spring tune-up to clean the coils.

6. Ignition or Pilot Problems (Furnaces)

Gas furnaces can develop ignition issues, such as a failing ignitor or a faulty flame sensor.

Warning signs: furnace won't light, cycles on and off, or a yellow flame rather than steady blue.

Cost: ignitor or flame sensor replacement runs $200 to $600. See our furnace repair page.

7. Dirty Ducts and Poor Airflow

Dust and debris can build up in ductwork over years, reducing airflow and circulating allergens.

Warning signs: uneven heating or cooling, dust around vents, or allergy symptoms worsening indoors.

Fix: professional duct cleaning restores airflow and air quality.

Prevention Is Cheaper Than Repair

Two tune-ups per year prevent most of these problems: a spring AC tune-up and a fall furnace tune-up. Annual maintenance catches small issues before they become expensive failures.
