Energy Saving HVAC Tips for Boise Homeowners

Boise homeowners pay for both a long heating season and a hot cooling season. These proven tips cut heating and cooling costs without sacrificing comfort.

Start With a Smart Thermostat

A smart thermostat is the highest-return upgrade for most Boise homes. It lowers the temperature while you sleep or are away and raises it before you return. Savings of 10% to 26% are typical. Most homeowners recover the cost in 1 to 2 years. See our thermostat installation guide.

Change Filters on Schedule

A clogged filter makes your system work harder and use more energy. In Boise's dry, dusty climate, replace the filter every 1 to 2 months during peak seasons. It is the cheapest energy-saving step you can take.

Keep Up With Annual Maintenance

A well-maintained system runs more efficiently. Two tune-ups per year, a spring AC tune-up and a fall furnace tune-up, keep the system at peak efficiency. Annual maintenance catches problems before they become expensive failures.

Block the Heat in Summer, Keep It In Winter

Close blinds on sunny windows during Boise's hottest afternoons. In winter, open curtains on sunny windows for free heat and close them at night. This simple habit cuts both heating and cooling demand.

Seal Leaks and Insulate

Air leaks around doors and windows waste conditioned air. Add weatherstripping and caulk gaps. Good insulation keeps heat out in summer and in during winter.

Use a Programmed Schedule

Avoid a constant temperature all day. Set a schedule that relaxes the temperature when no one is home. A smart thermostat does this automatically. Reducing the temperature by 7 to 10 degrees for 8 hours a day can cut costs by up to 10% a year.

Consider an Efficiency Upgrade at Replacement Time

When a system reaches the end of its life, an efficiency upgrade pays off. New AC units must meet a SEER2 rating of at least 13.4. Upgrading from an older 10 SEER unit to a 16 SEER2 unit can reduce cooling costs by roughly 37%. See our AC installation and furnace installation guides.

The Bottom Line

The cheapest energy is the energy you never use. Start with a smart thermostat and a clean filter. Add regular maintenance. Then tackle leaks and efficiency upgrades as your system ages.
