HVAC FAQ for Boise Homeowners

Got questions about heating, cooling, and HVAC service in Boise, Idaho? We've compiled answers to the most common questions we hear from Treasure Valley homeowners. If you don't find your answer here, call (208) 555-0123 or visit our contact page.

Air Conditioning Questions

How long does a central AC unit last in Boise?

Central air conditioners typically last 12 to 15 years. In Boise's hot, dry climate, that range can extend closer to 15 to 20 years with regular annual maintenance. Without maintenance — including filter changes and yearly tune-ups — expect the lower end or less. If your AC is over 12 years old and needs a major repair, replacement is usually the better investment. See our AC installation guide for more details.

What size AC do I need for my Boise home?

AC capacity is measured in tons (1 ton = 12,000 BTUs). A general guideline: 1,000 to 1,400 sq ft homes need 2 to 2.5 tons; 1,400 to 1,800 sq ft need 2.5 to 3 tons; 1,800 to 2,200 sq ft need 3 to 3.5 tons. However, the only accurate way to size a system is a professional Manual J load calculation, which factors in insulation, windows, ceiling height, sun exposure, and Boise's local climate data. An oversized AC short-cycles and doesn't control humidity; an undersized AC runs constantly and can't keep up on hot days.

Why is my AC freezing up in Boise's hot weather?

Despite the heat, AC evaporator coils can freeze when airflow is restricted. Common causes include a dirty air filter (a frequent problem in Boise's dusty climate), blocked return vents, a failing blower motor, or low refrigerant. When airflow drops too low, the coil temperature falls below freezing and ice forms. If you see ice on your refrigerant lines, turn off the AC and call a professional — running a frozen AC can damage the compressor.

How much does AC repair cost in Boise?

AC repair costs vary based on the problem. Minor repairs (capacitor, contactor, refrigerant top-off) typically cost $150 to $400. Major repairs (compressor, coil replacement) can range from $1,500 to $2,500+. Your technician will provide an upfront estimate before any work begins. Regular maintenance can prevent many costly repairs.

Furnace Questions

How long does a furnace last in Boise?

Gas furnaces typically last 15 to 25 years. With annual maintenance — including fall furnace tune-ups — many furnaces reach the upper end of that range. Without maintenance, the lifespan can be significantly shorter given Boise's long, cold heating season. If your furnace is over 15 years old and needs frequent repairs, check out our furnace installation guide.

Is a yellow pilot light dangerous?

Yes. A healthy gas furnace flame should be a steady blue. A yellow or flickering flame suggests incomplete combustion, which can produce carbon monoxide (CO) — a colorless, odorless, and potentially deadly gas. If your pilot light is yellow, or if your CO detector alarms, turn off the furnace, ventilate, evacuate, and call a licensed HVAC professional immediately. For more safety information, see our furnace repair page.

How often should I replace my furnace filter?

In Boise's dry, dusty climate, check your filter monthly and replace it as needed. During peak heating season (November to March) and peak cooling season (June to September), filters typically need replacement every 1 to 2 months. Homes with pets, allergy sufferers, or nearby construction may need more frequent changes. A clogged filter is the #1 cause of preventable HVAC breakdowns.

Should I repair or replace my furnace?

Use the $5,000 rule: multiply your furnace's age by the estimated repair cost. If the result exceeds $5,000, replacement is usually the better investment. For example, a 16-year-old furnace needing a $400 repair gives $6,400 — well over $5,000. Furnaces over 15 years old with a cracked heat exchanger should always be replaced rather than repaired, for safety reasons.

Maintenance and Efficiency Questions

How often should I get HVAC maintenance in Boise?

You should schedule two maintenance visits per year: a spring tune-up for your AC (March or April) and a fall tune-up for your furnace (September or October). Boise's climate demands both — we have a long cooling season and a long heating season, and both systems need to be ready before extreme weather arrives. Many contractors offer maintenance plans that include both visits at a discounted rate. See our HVAC maintenance page for more information.

Will a smart thermostat save me money in Boise?

Yes. Smart thermostats can save 10% to 26% on heating and cooling costs. In Boise's climate — with high heating bills in winter and high cooling bills in summer — those savings add up quickly. A smart thermostat automatically lowers the temperature while you're asleep or away and rises before you wake or return home. For most Boise homeowners, a smart thermostat pays for itself in 1 to 2 years. See our thermostat installation guide for details.

What is SEER2 and why does it matter?

SEER2 (Seasonal Energy Efficiency Ratio 2) measures how efficiently an air conditioner cools over a season. Higher SEER2 ratings mean lower energy bills. As of 2023, all new AC units must meet a minimum SEER2 rating of 13.4. Upgrading from an older 10 SEER unit to a 16 SEER2 unit can reduce cooling costs by roughly 37% — significant savings in a Boise summer. When shopping for a new AC, balance upfront cost against long-term efficiency savings.

Licensing and Service Questions

Do HVAC contractors need a license in Idaho?

Yes. Idaho law requires all HVAC contractors to be licensed through the Division of Occupational and Professional Licenses (DOPL). This includes completing an approved apprenticeship (576 hours of instruction plus 8,000 hours of supervised work), passing the state contractor's exam, and maintaining a $2,000 compliance bond. Always verify your contractor's license through the DOPL website before hiring. See our About page for more details on Idaho licensing.

What counts as an HVAC emergency?

An HVAC emergency is any situation that can't safely wait for the next business day — furnace failure during a cold snap, AC failure during extreme heat, suspected carbon monoxide leaks, burning smells, or water leaking from the AC unit. If you're unsure, call and a professional can help you determine whether emergency service is needed. See our emergency HVAC page for more information.

How fast can I get HVAC service in Boise?

For standard service calls, most contractors in our network can arrive within 1 to 2 business days. For emergencies, same-day service is often available — sometimes within a few hours. During peak heating and cooling seasons, wait times may be longer for non-emergency visits. Calling early in the season (before the first cold snap or heat wave) ensures the fastest scheduling.
