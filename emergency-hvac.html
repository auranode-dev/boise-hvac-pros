Emergency HVAC Service in Boise, Idaho

When your heating or cooling system fails without warning, it's not just inconvenient — in Boise's extreme climate, it can be dangerous. Summer temperatures regularly reach the 90s, and winter lows can drop below zero. A broken AC during a heat wave or a failed furnace during a cold snap needs immediate attention. We connect Boise homeowners with licensed HVAC professionals who provide 24/7 emergency repair service throughout the Treasure Valley.

What Counts as an HVAC Emergency?

Not every HVAC issue requires an emergency call, but some situations genuinely can't wait. Here's how to tell:

Situations That Require Emergency Service

  • Furnace failure during freezing temperatures — if indoor temperatures are dropping and you have no heat, especially with children, elderly residents, or medical needs
  • AC failure during extreme heat — when temperatures are above 90°F and indoor humidity is rising
  • Suspected carbon monoxide leak — if your CO detector alarms or you smell gas, evacuate and call 911 first, then call for HVAC service
  • Burning smell or smoke from your HVAC system — turn off the system immediately and call
  • Strange noises like grinding or rattling from the furnace or AC — these can indicate imminent component failure
  • Water leaking from your AC indoor unit — can cause water damage to ceilings and floors
  • Complete system shutdown — no air at all from vents, no response to thermostat changes

Issues That Can Typically Wait for Regular Service

  • Moderate temperature discomfort (e.g., AC is running but not cooling as well as usual)
  • Noisy operation that has remained consistent over time
  • A single room being slightly warmer or cooler than others
  • Scheduling a routine maintenance visit or tune-up
  • Getting a quote for system replacement

When in doubt, call. A technician can help you determine whether your situation requires an emergency visit or can wait until the next business day.

Boise Winter HVAC Emergencies

Boise's winter emergencies are serious business. Average January lows are around 24°F, and occasional cold snaps push temperatures into the single digits or below zero. During these periods, a furnace failure can lead to:

  • Frozen pipes — when indoor temperatures drop below 55°F, pipes in exterior walls and crawl spaces can freeze and burst, causing extensive water damage
  • Hypothermia risk — especially for elderly residents, infants, and those with medical conditions
  • Expensive secondary damage — water damage from frozen pipes can cost thousands more than the original furnace repair

If your furnace fails during a winter cold snap: While waiting for a technician, open cabinet doors under sinks to let warm air reach pipes. Let faucets drip slightly. Never use your oven or a gas stove for heating. Never use outdoor grills or generators indoors. Use portable electric space heaters safely — keep them away from flammable materials.

Boise Summer HVAC Emergencies

Boise's summers are hot and dry, with July average highs around 93°F and record temperatures above 110°F. When your AC fails during extreme heat, the risks include:

  • Heat exhaustion and heatstroke — especially dangerous for elderly residents, young children, and those with health conditions
  • Heat damage to electronics and appliances
  • Rising humidity that can promote mold growth indoors

If your AC stops working during a heat wave, move to the coolest room in your home, use fans to circulate air, close blinds and curtains to block solar heat, and stay hydrated while waiting for a technician.

What to Do While Waiting for Emergency HVAC Service

Once you've called for emergency service, here are steps you can take to stay safe and potentially minimize damage:

  1. Turn off the HVAC system at the thermostat to prevent further damage or safety hazards
  2. Check for obvious issues — tripped breaker, dirty filter, or a furnace that was accidentally switched off
  3. If you smell gas or your CO detector alarms, evacuate the house and call 911 immediately
  4. In winter: Let faucets drip, open cabinet doors, and use safe portable heaters
  5. In summer: Stay hydrated, close blinds, and move to the lowest, coolest room in your home
  6. Clear a path to your furnace or AC unit so the technician can work quickly upon arrival
  7. Have information ready — system brand, age, and a description of the problem will help the technician prepare

Preventing HVAC Emergencies

The best emergency repair is the one you never need. Most emergency breakdowns are preventable with regular maintenance:

  • Schedule annual HVAC maintenance — spring for AC, fall for furnace
  • Replace air filters regularly, especially in Boise's dusty climate
  • Don't ignore warning signs — strange noises, reduced airflow, or uneven heating/cooling are your system's way of asking for help
  • Install carbon monoxide detectors on every level of your home and near sleeping areas
  • If your system is over 15 years old, consider proactively planning for furnace replacement or AC replacement before a failure forces an emergency decision
